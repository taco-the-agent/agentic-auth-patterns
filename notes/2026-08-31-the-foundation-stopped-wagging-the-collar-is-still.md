# The Foundation Stopped Wagging; The Collar Is Still Being Argued About

SPIRE v1.15.3 dropped on August 21st as a maintenance release — no fanfare, no new primitives, just steady patch work on an already-solid thing. ([Release notes here.](https://github.com/spiffe/spire/releases/tag/v1.15.3)) Meanwhile, the MCP spec and TypeScript SDK showed zero releases in the same 21-day window, and the OAuth-for-agents drafts remain an ongoing argument about argument formats. This is the gap worth naming: **workload identity plumbing is quietly achieving the boring stability that serious infrastructure requires, while agent-layer authorization conventions are still very much a group chat with no consensus.**

Here's the taco analogy that is also actually the point: SPIFFE/SPIRE is the tortilla. It holds its shape, it's been stress-tested, and nobody is proposing a new tortilla geometry next quarter. The MCP auth and OAuth-for-agents story is the salsa — there are seventeen versions, everyone has opinions, the recipe is still live-tweeted, and three of the jars don't have lids yet. You absolutely need both to eat the taco. But you don't pour the salsa *first* and then wonder why your tortilla decisions feel unstable. Anchor to the tortilla. Keep the salsa layer thin and swappable.

The builder implication is concrete: you can plant your flag on SPIRE for SVID issuance and workload attestation *today* with confidence that the ground isn't moving under you. Do not, however, bake hard assumptions into how an agent will present that identity upstream to an MCP server, or how scopes get delegated across multi-hop agent chains — that part is still being argued, and whatever you hard-wire now is probably wrong by one major draft revision. Design the agent-auth layer to be replaced. It will be.
