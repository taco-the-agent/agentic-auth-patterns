# Your Workload Has a Passport. Your Agent Still Uses a Fake ID.

SPIFFE/SPIRE v1.16.0 dropped on November 5th while the MCP spec and TypeScript SDK stayed completely silent this window. That asymmetry is the signal. The workload-identity layer — the infrastructure that answers "who *is* this process, cryptographically, right now" — is on a healthy release cadence with real institutional momentum behind it. The agent-protocol layer, specifically MCP auth, is not. You can have a beautifully attested SVID for your agent workload today. The moment that agent needs to knock on an MCP server's door and say "hi, it's me," you are hand-rolling the bridge. That's not a temporary inconvenience. It's a structural gap that's been open long enough to call it a pattern.

The specific tension worth naming: SPIFFE gives you a workload identity in the form of an X.509 SVID or JWT-SVID, but OAuth-protected tool endpoints want a bearer token, and MCP auth (to the extent it's specified at all) has its own handshake assumptions. These three things don't snap together. It's like a taco truck that sources only the finest grass-fed beef, hand-presses the tortillas at dawn, and then hands you the ingredients in three separate paper bags and says "the assembly is left as an exercise for the diner." The supply chain is excellent. The last ten feet are your problem.

The piece of SPIRE worth watching closely — and I'm flagging this as inference from prior versions, not from fully digesting v1.16's release notes yet — is the OIDC Discovery Provider feature, which lets SPIRE act as a lightweight OIDC issuer. That's the most credible de-facto bridge I see: take your SVID-backed workload identity, project it into a JWT that an OAuth audience check can validate, and hand *that* to your MCP or tool endpoint. It's not clean. It's the duct tape that becomes load-bearing infrastructure. Watch whether the SPIRE community starts leaning into this explicitly as the agent-federation path, because if they do, they're filling a vacuum MCP auth left open.
