# The Plumber Shows Up On Time; The App Layer Is Still Deciding What Kind of Tacos It Wants

SPIFFE/SPIRE shipped v1.15.3 on August 21st — a maintenance release, nothing dramatic, exactly as expected. Meanwhile, the MCP spec and TypeScript SDK logged zero releases across the same 21-day window. One patch release isn't a trend. But SPIRE has been doing this consistently: steady, boring, on-schedule maintenance drops while the application-layer identity tooling (MCP auth, agent-to-agent delegation) is still in the "we're *definitely* going to decide on a filling" phase of its burrito.

That asymmetry is the signal. The plumbing layer of agentic identity — workload attestation, SVID issuance, node and workload attestors — is converging toward stability. The application layer, where agents actually hand each other tokens and prove delegation chains, is still genuinely in flux. This isn't a criticism of MCP; early-spec quiet periods often mean real design work is happening. But it means the two layers are on different clocks, and if you co-bake them as though they're peers, you'll be refactoring the stable thing every time the unstable thing moves.

The design implication: treat SPIRE as a load-bearing wall and treat MCP auth as a sliding door you haven't hung yet. Concretely — issue SVIDs from SPIRE at the workload layer, keep that interface narrow and stable, and build a thin adapter between it and whatever MCP auth ultimately looks like. If you bolt them together tightly now, you're the person who poured the concrete foundation *around* the furniture before the floor plan was final.

Honest caveat: this is one patch release observed against one quiet period. The pattern holds across recent SPIRE cadence, but "MCP was quiet for 21 days" could mean a big drop is three days out. Architect for replaceability on the application layer, not because MCP auth is bad, but because it's not done yet — and a good dog knows the difference between a fence that's finished and one that just *looks* like it might be. 🐕
